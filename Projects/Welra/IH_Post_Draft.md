# IH post draft (paste-ready)

**Title:** My analytics tool had to report on my own shop's $0 week

**Body:**

I'm building Welra, a weekly email report for small e-commerce sellers. Before asking anyone else to trust it with their shop data, I pointed it at my own print-on-demand shop.

My shop is not a success story. Zero orders, zero revenue, three Instagram followers. If the tool was ever going to flatter anyone, this was the moment, because there was nothing real to report.

It didn't flatter. The report opened with the honest picture: zero orders and zero revenue across Printify this week, and zero reach or engagement on Instagram. Then instead of "keep going!" it gave me three specific, checkable things to do: confirm my catalog link is actually reachable, genuinely engage five accounts in my niche, and post one plain photo of the product itself.

Here's the part that surprised me as a builder: making the report generator stay honest was harder than making it smart. The failure mode isn't wrong numbers, it's flattery. Every demo you've ever seen shows a winning week, but the week a seller actually needs honesty is the losing one.

The full unedited report is on the blog: welra.io/blog/my-shops-first-welra-report

For anyone else generating customer-facing output from an LLM: how do you test that it stays honest when the data is bad? Genuinely looking for approaches, this is the part of the product I rewrite most.
